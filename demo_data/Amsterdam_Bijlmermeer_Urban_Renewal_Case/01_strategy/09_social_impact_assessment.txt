SOCIAL IMPACT ASSESSMENT — ILLUSTRATIVE BASELINE

This synthetic assessment asks who benefits, who carries disruption, and what must be protected during renewal. It assumes a diverse resident population, including families, older residents, young adults, and households with mobility or care needs. No demographic figure in this document is a real survey result.

POTENTIAL BENEFITS

Safer routes, warmer homes, better access to community space, more varied housing choice, local employment during construction, and more usable green space.

POTENTIAL HARMS

Temporary relocation, rent pressure, loss of informal social networks, construction noise, disruption to school routes, and the risk that a new public-realm image is prioritised over resident needs.

MITIGATION COMMITMENTS FOR THE CASE

Maintain a resident liaison point; publish a plain-language phasing map; offer a return or continuity pathway; monitor rent and displacement indicators; reserve community space in the first delivery package; and require a post-occupancy review at 6 and 18 months.

Decision: no option proceeds to detailed design without a resident continuity plan and a disruption budget.
